\begin{abstract}
Search, branching, and candidate selection can improve the trajectories used to
train language-model policies, but they also change which states and actions
reach the optimizer.  The ordinary PPO ratio corrects policy-version drift; it
does not generally correct the allocation, generation, and retention law of a
search procedure.  We formalize this \emph{search-induced policy mismatch},
separate the raw-Actor objective from the retained composite-search objective,
and compare no correction, joint trajectory propensity correction,
executed-action marginalization, partial correction, and a retained-candidate
objective.  On exact finite reasoning trees, full correction removes bias but
can sharply reduce effective sample size; a development-frozen partial
correction minimizes gradient MSE at smaller batches, while the MSE-optimal
correction strength shifts toward full correction as batch size grows.  Critic ranking quality predicts whether induced shift is
useful, whereas shift magnitude alone does not.  A pre-specified, hash-frozen three-seed
Qwen3-4B study on MATH-500 finds no resolved transfer advantage for the
corrected raw Actors or retained-composite objective, and all 72 search-training
branches fail the original sequence-product stability gate.  A prospective
two-seed low-update confirmation finds selected-token clip rates below one
percent, yet no resolved \Eone{} or \Ethree{} advantage at either H8 or H16;
longer decoding raises answer coverage and accuracy without changing that
conclusion.  Cross-task stress tests on ARC-Challenge likewise find only
sub-percentage-point unresolved effects, while an open-ended DROP test finds the
raw \Efour{} Actor worse than all three registered baselines.  A two-seed
segmentation diagnostic increases the number of search decisions without
improving retained-composite accuracy.  Together these results support
target-aware accounting and variance control, not a universal winning
correction or automatic scale-up.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Search-augmented reinforcement learning (RL) is often described as collecting
better on-policy data: candidates are sampled from the current Actor, a search
or scorer retains promising continuations, and PPO updates the Actor using the
usual new-to-old policy ratio~\citep{schulman2017ppo}.  The first clause does
not imply the last.  Search changes the multiplicity of visited prefixes, the
probability that an action survives selection, and the downstream occupancy of
states.  Consequently, the retained training distribution can differ from the
raw Actor even when every primitive generation call uses that Actor.

This distinction matters because two reasonable objectives coexist.  One may
wish to distill an improved search policy, in which case selection bias is part
of the intended teacher distribution.  Alternatively, one may claim to
estimate or improve the return of the deployed no-search Actor, in which case
the same selected data require a target-aware correction.  Correct
response-to-token credit assignment does not by itself correct search
retention, and propensity correction does not repair an inaccurate continuation
Value.

We make four contributions.  First, we decompose search-generated data into
allocation, candidate generation, retention, and temporal credit, and define a
target-explicit estimator taxonomy.  Second, a frozen exact-tree matrix provides
enumerated ground truth for bias, gradient MSE, effective sample size (ESS),
occupancy shift, and post-update return.  Third, a pre-specified, hash-frozen MATH-500 study
tests three search families under both no-search raw-Actor deployment and
retained-composite inference, retaining failed gates and negative contrasts; a
prospective low-update confirmation separates sequence-product and token-level
clipping while repeating the estimator contrast at H8 and H16.  Fourth,
task-specific ARC-Challenge and DROP studies test whether the same findings
persist across task formats while probing answer coverage, horizon choice, and
decision granularity.  The contribution is a target-aware empirical account,
not a claim that one estimator universally wins.  Our contribution is a
target-aware formalization and matched empirical audit of the behavior law
induced by search-augmented LLM RL; we use classical weighting, marginalization,
and shrinkage tools to isolate its statistical and optimization consequences.

\section{Problem formulation}

\subsection{Two target objectives}

Let $\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$ be the raw Actor and let $R(\tau)$ be the terminal
return of trajectory $\tau$.  The deployed no-search objective is
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{actor}}(\theta)
  = \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_{\pi_\theta}}[R(\tau)].
  \label{eq:raw-objective}
\end{equation}
A search controller additionally chooses an allocation decision $g$, generates
a candidate set $C$, and retains an action or continuation through $q$.  These
operations induce a composite trajectory law $p_{\mathcal{S}_\phi(\pi_\theta)}$
and a distinct objective
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{search}}(\theta,\phi)
  = \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_{\mathcal{S}_\phi(\pi_\theta)}}[R(\tau)].
  \label{eq:search-objective}
\end{equation}
Optimizing Equation~\ref{eq:search-objective} can be useful, but it is not an
unbiased estimator of Equation~\ref{eq:raw-objective} merely because candidate
tokens came from $\pi_\theta$.

\subsection{The missing search ratio}
Let \(x\) be the retained state--action history and \(z\) latent allocation,
candidate-set, and retention events.  If \(\mu_{\rm old}\) is the old search
behavior, the complete raw-Actor ratio factors as
\begin{equation}
\frac{p_{\pi_\theta}(x)}{\mu_{\rm old}(x)}
=\underbrace{\frac{p_{\pi_\theta}(x)}{p_{\pi_{\rm old}}(x)}}_{\text{policy drift}}
 \underbrace{\frac{p_{\pi_{\rm old}}(x)}{\mu_{\rm old}(x)}}_{\text{search mismatch}}.
\label{eq:ratio-factorization}
\end{equation}
Ordinary PPO addresses the first factor locally, not the second. If search
removes support, reweighting retained samples cannot recover missing histories.

For normalized \(\rho(z\mid x)\), let
\(W_{\rm joint}=p_\pi(x)\rho(z\mid x)/\mu(x,z)\) and
\(W_{\rm marg}=p_\pi(x)/\mu(x)\).
\begin{proposition}[Exactness and marginalization]\leavevmode\par
Both recover \(\mathbb E_{p_\pi}[h]\) for square-integrable \(h(x)\), and
\(\operatorname{Var}_\mu(W_{\rm marg}h)\le
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(W_{\rm joint}h)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proposition}[Augmentation variance]
Conditioned on \(x\),
\(\mathbb E_{\mu(z\mid x)}[W_{\rm joint}^2h^2]
=W_{\rm marg}^2h^2[1+\chi^2(\rho(\cdot\mid x)\Vert\mu(\cdot\mid x))]\).
Thus the marginal weight minimizes the conditional second moment.
\end{proposition}
Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. In \Eone{}, IID Actor-drawn candidate
positions yield logged factor \(1/(Kq_i)\) for selector probability \(q_i\);
\Etwo{} marginalizes latent candidate paths when computable.

\paragraph{Three axes.}
Allocation selects prefixes for compute; retention selects candidates for the
update; credit assignment supplies their advantages. Better credit does not
undo allocation or retention propensity.

\Ezero{} treats selected actions as Actor data. \Eone{} multiplies inverse
selection propensities; \Etwo{} Rao--Blackwellizes \Eone{} by marginalizing
search events. \Ethree{} uses
\(W_t^{(\lambda)}=\prod_{j\le t}[(1-\lambda)+\lambda w_j]\) with frozen
\(\lambda=.75\), trading bias for finite-batch variance. The fixed estimator
does not become unbiased with batch size; the MSE-optimal family member can
move toward \(\lambda=1\). \Efour{} targets \(J_{\rm search}\), not
\(J_{\rm actor}\).

\subsection{Exact correction versus clipped PPO}
Our implementation puts external search weight \(W_t\) outside the PPO clip:
\begin{equation}
L_{\rm clip}=\mathbb E_\mu[W_t\min\{r_tA_t,
\operatorname{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t\}].
\label{eq:clipped-surrogate}
\end{equation}
Clipping, estimated advantages, Value/KL losses, and optimization mean that an
exact \(W_t\) does not make the realized update unbiased. We call an estimator
exact only for its stated unclipped expectation under support and correct logs.

\section{Exact-tree evidence}

\subsection{Frozen design}

The confirmation matrix contains 30 committed finite MDPs, nine Critic
conditions, and three search families: always-expanded best-of-2,
entropy-gated K4, and low-Value-gated K4.  Each of the 810
MDP--Critic--family cells uses ten manifested common-random-number streams and
4,096 trajectories per stream.  Exact enumeration supplies raw-Actor and
composite gradients, state-action occupancy, and return.  Development fixes all
thresholds and $\lambda$ before confirmation.  Inference clusters by MDP rather
than treating repeated Critic conditions as independent problems.

\subsection{Bias, variance, and ESS}

Under the exact Critic, \Ezero{} has mean relative raw-gradient bias of 36.91\%
for best-of-2, 24.94\% for entropy-gated K4, and 23.41\% for low-Value-gated
K4.  Both \Eone{} and \Etwo{} recover the exact target in expectation, but
\Etwo{} has substantially lower batch-256 gradient MSE because it marginalizes
latent search paths.  The development-frozen \Ethree{} has lower batch-256 MSE
than both \Ezero{} and full \Etwo{} in all 27 Critic-by-family conditions.  At
batch 1,024, the optimum moves toward full correction: \Ethree{} remains better
for best-of-2, is unresolved for entropy-gated K4, and is slightly worse than
\Etwo{} for the low-Value gate.  A post-hoc CPU-only decomposition of the
frozen 810 cells using
$\operatorname{MSE}(n)=\lVert\operatorname{Bias}\rVert^2+
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)/n$ makes the transition explicit: \Ethree{} has
lower descriptive MSE in 765 cells at $n=256$, 491 at $n=1{,}024$, and only 98
at $n=4{,}096$; the median cellwise crossover is $n=1{,}350$.  These
crossovers inherit Monte Carlo error and are diagnostic rather than a new
pre-specified confirmation.

\subsection{Useful shift versus large shift}

The exact Critic yields positive search gain, while the inverted Critic creates
similar occupancy-shift magnitude but negative return effects.  Across exact,
lagged, learned, noisy, and inverted Critics, ranking concordance tracks gain
direction more closely than maximum-depth total variation.  Scalar Value and
entropy are therefore incomplete value-of-computation signals: a state may
need correction without offering diverse corrective actions, or offer diverse
actions when correction is unnecessary.

The frozen compute-matched gate study makes this limitation explicit.  At K4
and expected-candidate budget 2.0, entropy gating improves composite return over
random allocation by $+0.01575$ with a problem-clustered 95\% interval
$[+0.00298,+0.02944]$.  The corresponding scalar-Value and
Value--entropy-gate differences are $+0.00079$
$[-0.01553,+0.01639]$ and $+0.00594$ $[-0.00670,+0.01874]$; neither is
resolved.  A development-frozen linear gate using low Value, entropy, and their
interaction has near-zero held-out correlation with oracle expansion gain.
Policy-weighted dispersion and shape of per-Action Critic values are highly
predictive diagnostics, but require already generated candidates, an
uncertainty head, or an ensemble and therefore are not treated as free
deployment signals.  Thus the intuitive four quadrants explain distinct
``need'' and ``opportunity'' failure modes, but are not themselves an oracle
value-of-computation rule.

\section{Controlled LLM transfer}

\subsection{Pre-specified design}

We use Qwen3-4B and three training seeds.  Each seed shares a 48-problem
burn-in, followed by two 48-problem adaptive rounds for best-of-$N$,
entropy--Value branching, and Value/PRM rollout (hereafter,
Value/PRM).  The estimator axis is
\Ezero, \Eone, \Ethree, and \Efour.  All training completes before a sealed
200-problem MATH-500 scalar partition is opened.  We evaluate 39 no-search raw
K1 Actors and 36 retained composite systems, always on the same ordered problem
IDs.  Candidate tokens, candidate forwards, selected tokens, registered target
expansion, actual expansion on the round-two threshold-source rollout, actual
sealed-evaluation expansion, wall time, GPU time, ESS, maximum weight, clip fraction, KL, Value
calibration, and surface-form diversity are retained for audit.
We define answer rate as the fraction of examples for which the frozen strict
\texttt{FINAL:} extractor recovers a scalar answer within eight generation
steps; it is not an explicit abstention or refusal probability.  We therefore
also report accuracy conditional on a valid final and audit whether unanswered
transcripts lack any final-like wording or use only noncanonical final-like
wording.
Raw-K1 decoding is greedy, whereas retained-composite candidate generation uses
the frozen temperature 1.3 and common sampling seed 100901 for paired
comparison.  Answer rate is therefore an empirical coverage statistic in both
tables, not a per-problem abstention probability estimated by the model.

\Etwo{} is evaluated on the exact trees but omitted from the LLM matrix because
its executed-action marginal requires summing all candidate-set and selection
paths that can produce the retained text.  That sum is exactly enumerable in
the finite study but not available from the frozen LLM logger.  The omission is
therefore a computability boundary, not an outcome-based estimator choice.

For the original S3 study, paired wins/losses/ties are computed problem
by problem.  Ten thousand problem-clustered bootstrap resamples preserve all
three training-seed observations for each sampled problem, and family-wise
tests use Holm adjustment.  These intervals quantify problem uncertainty
conditional on three fixed seeds; they are not precise population intervals
over training seeds.

\subsection{Frozen stability and prospective low-update confirmation}

The prospectively frozen S5 study uses only the best-of-2 search family (two
candidates, temperature 1.3, epsilon 0.10, beta 1.0, and full expansion) plus a
K1 control; it does not mix the entropy--Value or Value/PRM search families.

All 72 original search-training branches exceed the pre-specified
sequence-product clip-fraction ceiling of 0.20.  This remains a failed
registered gate and prohibited automatic scale-up.  The diagnostic is not a
token-drop rate: only sign-aligned outside-band samples reach the flat portion
of the clipped Actor surrogate, while oppositely signed samples retain a
corrective gradient; Value, entropy, and KL terms may also remain active.

\section{Related work}
Adaptive branching allocates compute by entropy, Value, process scores, or
information gain \citep{he2026bpo,xing2025latr,khomich2026epigtree,li2026patr}.
Current-policy candidates do not imply retained histories follow raw-Actor
occupancy. Search distillation and expert iteration deliberately train toward a
planner \citep{djuhera2026tsr,feng2023tsllm,zhang2024restmcts,anthony2017expert,silver2017alphazero};
we treat this as a legitimate different target.

Off-policy objectives distinguish behavior and target occupancy, while
conditional importance sampling, doubly robust estimation, and shrinkage
control variance \citep{degris2012offpolicy,imani2018emphatic,liu2020conditional,jiang2016doubly,kallus2020double,munos2016retrace,espeholt2018impala}.
Our contribution is to isolate the behavior law induced jointly by allocation,
candidate generation, and retention and compare target-matched estimators.
Continuation methods improve credit but do not correct retention by themselves
\citep{kazemnejad2024vineppo,he2025trepo}.

\section{Conclusion}
Search changes the data-generating law before PPO sees a token.  Correcting the
appropriate target is therefore a statistical estimand choice, not a generic
recipe for improvement.  Exact trees show that full correction removes bias but
can incur severe variance, while marginalization and partial correction improve
finite-sample behavior.  Across the frozen language-model studies, neither the
original matrix nor the prospective low-update confirmation with token-level
stability diagnostics establishes a
resolved corrected-Actor advantage.  Longer horizons repair answer coverage but
not estimator ordering.  The practical recommendation is to name the
deployment target, log search propensities, distinguish sequence diagnostics
from token-level clipping, and require stability and uncertainty checks before
scaling.

\section{Limitations and broader impact}
Exact trees lack large-model realism; three original LLM seeds and two S5
confirmation seeds limit optimizer-level inference, and S5 does not include \Efour{}. MATH scalar extraction,
ARC multiple choice, and DROP QA omit code and tools. H8 is coverage limited
and H16 diagnostic. ARC/DROP test task-specific raw Actors; segmentation is
post hoc. Failed gates and negative/unresolved results preclude universal
\method{} or safe-scale-up claims. Stronger reasoning retains standard risks.
